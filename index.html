Act 1 Dialogue Excerpts

Read how the 21 target words drive the prologue, public brawls, marriage politics, and Queen Mab's speech.

The Prologue Chorus
"Two households, both alike in dignity,
In fair Verona, where we lay our scene,
From ancient grudge break to new mutiny,
Where civil blood makes civil hands unclean."

Context Analysis: Shakespeare uses the double meaning of civil (citizens of the same town vs. polite/peaceful behavior) to expose the tragic absurdity of citizen neighbors slaughtering each other.

Act 1, Scene 1 Tybalt confronts Benvolio
"What, drawn, and talk of peace? I hate the word,
As I hate hell, all Montagues, and thee.
Have at thee, coward!"

Context Analysis: Tybalt treats Benvolio as his sworn adversary, proving that the ancient feud has poisoned even the polite manners of Verona's young nobility.

Act 1, Scene 2 Lord Capulet to Paris
"My will to her consent is but a part;
An she agree, within her scope of choice
Lies my consent and fair according voice."

Context Analysis: Capulet momentarily insists that Juliet's own consent is required for marriage, showing parental merit and care before later forcing the match.
